# Concept brief: two new Livistone locations (Eye of Winter Gate and Eyelense Gate)

This brief is for an image-generation model or a concept artist. Please make **concept images** for two new landmarks in Livistone. The owner approves the concepts first; only after that are the landmarks built in the browser game.

## Latest owner direction and approval — 4 October 2026

The Winter requirements below are the original exploration brief; the owner has superseded its two open arches and single blue stone. **Approved:** [Winter G with its shutter open](../concepts/15-moon-gates/images/17-winter-g-model-two-stones-open.png). Preserve the actual ring model's geometry and proportions, using uniform scale and a horizontal rigid orientation. Add the large clear domed front stone and the smaller blue faceted hexagonal stone set behind it, as in the original front/back/side photographs. The building is an enclosed eye, with a lower-front hexagonal camera-style shutter entrance and enough room to stand and walk inside. Do not make a permanent through-hole or turn the entire eye into opaque blue crystal. Implementation is authorized; see [concept decisions](../concepts/15-moon-gates/notes.md).

Eyelense's enlarged red bead as a walk-through doorway is accepted as a direction; no individual variant has been selected.

## 1. What Livistone is

Livistone is a browser-playable 3D town that you walk through in first person. It is an art-and-science fantasy settlement, and **every landmark is an inhabitable or walk-through interpretation of a real jewelry piece by Livia Zaharia**.

- **Built so far:**
  - The Ministry of Energy, an amber cup inside folded silver (the Mitoring ring).
  - The Ministry of Science, a glass dome in a silver urchin lattice (the Nanot pendant).
  - City Hall, a walnut-and-crystal sphere (the Nut of Power).
  - Embryo Station, an amber roof clasped by a pierced silver ring.
  - The King's Chapel ring, as the city gate.
- **Look:**
  - Polished silver and warm amber, with white organic garden architecture.
  - Stone-paved paths 2.6 m wide with low kerbs, meadows, oak and ash woodland, a river valley ringed by mountains.
  - Daylight is a physical sky with cumulus clouds; night has stars, a moon and glowing jewels.
  - It feels like a calm, lush mountain valley town.
- **Rule for the jewelry:** each building is the jewel *translated into architecture*, not the jewel scaled up. The silhouette, structural logic and materials of the piece must be recognisable, but the result has to read as a place people walk through: thresholds, paving, a human scale, gardens round it.

### What went wrong before

An attempt to place Livia's 3D print files directly in the town (`concepts/15-moon-gates/images/`) was rejected. Those were jewelry meshes scaled to 10 m and dropped on a meadow: no paving, no garden, no architectural reading, and the Eyelense even had the wrong colour. **Do not just enlarge the jewel.** Design a landmark that grows out of it.

## 2. Where they go

Both sites are open, gently rolling meadows on the town side of the river, with woodland around them. The surrounding trees will be cleared as needed.

| Landmark | Site | Approach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Eye of Winter Gate** | West meadow, beyond the Ministry of Energy (amber and silver), with the western mountain ridges behind | A paved garden path arrives from the town (east) |
| **Eyelense Gate** | East strip, beyond the Ministry of Science (glass dome in a silver lattice), with the eastern ridges behind | A paved garden path arrives from the town (west) |

Each gate should feel like the destination of a short walk out of town into the meadows, a "different perspective" stop. Each needs a small paved forecourt, planting or water that belongs to the theme, and room to stand back and look.

## 3. Eye of Winter Gate: a double moon gate

### The jewel (facts, from Livia's catalogue)

- **Eye of Winter Double Ring and Pendant**, 2026, collection *A world for everyone* (Romanian Jewelry Week 2026).
- Materials: sterling silver, faceted topaz, quartz. Size 4.2 × 4 × 3.5 cm. It can be worn as a double ring or as a pendant.
- **Photographs:** `public/images/jewelry/catalogue/eye-of-winter-1.webp` and `eye-of-winter-2.webp`.
- **Shape:**
  - Two open silver finger rings sit side by side and cross at the centre, like a figure-eight.
  - Each ring ends in a small flared trumpet-shaped stone cup.
  - Below the crossing, two slender silver leaf-shaped bezels end in small round screw eyes.
  - The top (pendant) part is a pale-blue topaz cabochon held in an **irregular honeycomb cage of polished silver**: open hexagonal cells wrapping the stone.

### Story (Livia Lore: fiction, keep it labelled as such)

In Livia Lore the Eye of Winter freezes the area around it. In the town this becomes a small zone of winter that fades into the green meadow. It is fiction, never a claim about the real piece.

### Design brief

- **Exactly two** adjacent, circular, **walk-through** openings, derived from the two finger rings:
  - Each opening about 4 m clear, side by side, joined at a narrow shared support where the rings cross.
  - Not concentric, and not one behind the other.
  - Both openings must be plainly open at ground level for walking.
- The two rings may splay slightly, as on the jewel, and end in their flared trumpet terminals.
- **Above the shared join:** the pale-blue translucent stone in its **open honeycomb silver cage**. It ties both openings together and blocks neither.
- **Path:** the approach path splits into two level paths, one through each opening, which rejoin in the winter garden beyond.
- **Winter, localised:**
  - snow on the nearest trees and shrubs
  - icicles on the silver
  - a shallow pool frozen to ice
  - frost veins radiating across the paving

  All of it fades out into the green meadow within about 15–20 m. Beyond that, the rest of the valley is in full summer.
- **Materials:** polished silver (the town's silver), a pale-blue translucent topaz, and warm-white stone paving with low kerbs. Optional low curved white wall wings, as in the earlier study.
- **Scale:** about 12–14 m wide and 8–10 m tall overall. Pedestrians are visible for scale.

## 4. Eyelense Gate: the broken-lens gate

### The jewel (facts, from Livia's catalogue and the public pieces page)

- **Eyelense Pendant**, 2026, collection *A world for everyone* (Romanian Jewelry Week 2026).
- Materials: PLA (3D-printed plastic), glass, brass; the lens is heliomatic, meaning colour-changing (photochromic). Size 7 × 3 × 2.5 cm.
- **Photographs:** `public/images/jewelry/catalogue/eyelense-1.webp` and `eyelense-2.webp`.
- **Artist's text, quoted (it may be paraphrased in captions):** "Story of a broken lens. When one colour-changing sunglass lens breaks, both usually have to be replaced. Livia decided to make something with them." Silver/brass screws thread the assembly together.
- **Shape and colour (look at the photos):**
  - A thick, **glossy black** crescent, like an eyelid or a breaking wave, carved with raised swirling relief.
  - It wraps around a clear, curved **sunglass lens** that fills the eye of the crescent.
  - Several **brass rings** are threaded through holes along its upper edge.
  - Inside the eye hangs a small **clear glass ring holding a red glass bead**, suspended on a brass wire.
  - **It is black, not gold.** The brass appears only as rings and wire.

### Story

A broken lens given a second life: a frame that looks out at the town. In Livistone fiction, the lens darkens in bright daylight and clears at dusk, just as the real heliomatic lens does. That is the gate's "living" behaviour, and it is fiction about the place, not a product claim.

### Design brief

- **The black crescent stands upright as the gate frame:**
  - It is a great curved wall or arch with the raised swirl relief.
  - Its feet rest on a paved forecourt; one heavier side, as on the jewel, is fine.
  - Visitors **walk under and through the eye**: the opening needs at least 3.5 m clear height and about 4 m clear width at ground level.
- **The lens:**
  - A very large, gently curved, transparent tinted glass sheet in the upper part of the eye, high above people's heads, so it frames the town and sky without blocking the way.
  - Show it **tinted (smoky grey-brown) in the daylight concept**.
  - Optionally add a second **dusk or night** image with the lens clear and the red bead glowing softly.
- **Brass rings:** large brass rings threaded through holes along the crest of the crescent, as on the jewel.
- **The bead:** a red glass bead inside a clear glass ring, suspended from the crest on a brass rod or cable, hanging in the eye above the walkers.
- **Materials:** glossy black (a printed or lacquered stone-like surface with the swirl relief), brass, clear and tinted glass, red glass. The forecourt is the town's warm-white stone paving with low kerbs.
- **Setting:** a quiet viewing place. Benches or a low wall to sit and look through the lens; meadow flowers. No winter here.
- **Scale:** about 10–12 m across and 8–10 m tall, with pedestrians for scale.

## 5. Constraints that make the concepts buildable

The game has to build these in code and run them in a browser, on phones too. Please respect the following:

- **Walkable:** every opening meets a level paved path at ground level, with no steps or lips. The 2.6 m paths must pass straight through the openings.
- **Readable silhouette** from about 60 m away across the meadow, with the jewel recognisable.
- **Moderate detail:**
  - Bold primary forms with a limited number of secondary elements (honeycomb cells, rings, relief).
  - No dense filigree that only reads up close.
  - Snow, ice and frost are surface effects and a few icicles, not a field of crystals.
- **No text, logos, signage or watermarks** in the images; the game adds its own signs.
- **Same palette and light** as the town style references: a soft mountain-valley day with cumulus clouds.

## 6. Deliverables

For each gate:

1. **Hero view:** eye level, about 15–20 m from the gate on the approach path, both openings (Eye of Winter) or the eye with its lens (Eyelense) fully visible, people for scale.
2. **Three-quarter view:** showing depth, the forecourt and how the path passes through.
3. **Aerial context view:** the gate in its meadow with the edge of the town, matching the in-game map camera (high, looking down at about 45°).
4. **Eyelense only, optional:** a dusk or night view with the clear lens and the glowing red bead.

Landscape 3:2 or 16:9, at least 1536 px wide, PNG.
